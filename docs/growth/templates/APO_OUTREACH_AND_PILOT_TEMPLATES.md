# APO Outreach And Pilot Templates

Last updated: May 8, 2026

## Proof-Pack Positioning

APO should be described as an automation-risk forecasting and transition-planning product that turns occupation, task, skill, resume, and workforce data into reviewable career-resilience artifacts.

Use these templates only when the named proof pack can be shown on a current route or sample artifact. Do not imply production deployment, guaranteed career outcomes, regulatory approval, Lightcast equivalence, or GPU/model superiority.

## LinkedIn Content Templates

### Post 1: Automation Anxiety To Transition Plan

Most "will AI replace my job?" tools stop at the risk score.

That is not enough.

The useful workflow is:

1. Which tasks are exposed?
2. Which human judgment remains valuable?
3. Which adjacent roles are realistic?
4. Which skill gaps matter most?
5. What can someone do in the next 30-90 days?

I am building Automation Insights around that full chain: automation exposure -> bridge roles -> skill gaps -> career resilience report.

### Post 2: Coach Report Monetization

Career coaches are being pushed upstream.

Resume edits and cover letters are becoming commodity AI tasks. The higher-value coaching work is helping clients understand where their work is going and how to reposition before a crisis.

That is why I am building white-label career resilience reports: automation exposure, task drivers, transition paths, and skill gaps that a coach can discuss with a client.

### Post 3: Utilities Workforce

Utilities do not just need more technology. They need workforce transition planning for grid modernization.

The skills pressure is specific: cybersecurity, SCADA/OT, data science, DER, GIS, regulatory reporting, safety, and field operations.

I am exploring a utility workforce audit that maps roles to automation exposure, transition paths, and reskilling ROI. It is workforce planning, not load forecasting.

## Qualification Gates

- Buyer can name one current client, learner, workforce, or department workflow.
- Buyer can name the decision owner or review owner.
- A visible APO route or sample artifact can demonstrate the proof pack today.
- The pilot can use public examples, anonymized role lists, or buyer-supplied data with explicit source disclosure.
- The artifact is one of: career resilience report, resume AI-proof score card, bridge-role plan, skill-adjacency map, utility workforce audit, methods appendix.
- Reject or park the lead if the buyer needs production HRIS approval, legal/compliance guarantees, regulator approval, guaranteed placement outcomes, or enterprise data-provider equivalence.

## Claim Boundary Snippets

- "This is a pilot review, not a production HRIS deployment."
- "This is O*NET-backed analysis with AI synthesis; O*NET itself is not an automation-risk forecast."
- "The route is useful for review, but we still separate source-implemented features from fully proven runtime behavior."
- "The utility package is workforce planning, not load forecasting, SCADA, ADMS, or power-flow analysis."
- "The report is a coaching artifact, not a guarantee of job safety or placement."

## Do-Not-Say Guardrails

- Do not claim production customers.
- Do not claim guaranteed risk reduction.
- Do not claim guaranteed jobs, salaries, or interviews.
- Do not claim regulatory approval.
- Do not claim Lightcast equivalence.
- Do not claim GPU superiority.
- Do not imply O*NET alone proves automation risk.
- Do not imply the enterprise dashboard has real HRIS persistence until verified.
- Do not imply resume privacy/deletion guarantees beyond implemented controls.
