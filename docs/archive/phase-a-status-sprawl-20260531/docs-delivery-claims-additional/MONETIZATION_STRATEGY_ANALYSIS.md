2. **Personalization wins** - Generic career advice is commoditized
3. **Data moat matters** - O*NET + Gemini integration is defensible
4. **Retention > Acquisition** - Focus on keeping customers, not just getting them

### What the Research MISSES

1. **Platform risk** - Whop-only strategy is dangerous
2. **Pricing psychology** - $29/mo is awkward (too high for individuals, too low for businesses)
3. **Sales motion** - B2B requires different go-to-market than B2C
4. **Churn drivers** - Career tools have seasonal usage patterns (job search cycles)

### My Contrarian Takes

#### 1. Skip Whop Initially
**Reasoning:** You have a production-ready app that can monetize TODAY. Whop integration adds 6-8 weeks of dev time with uncertain ROI. Launch direct B2C first, prove monetization, THEN expand to Whop.

#### 2. Charge More
**Reasoning:** $29/mo positions you as a "nice-to-have" tool. $49/mo for Pro, $149/mo for Enterprise signals premium value and attracts serious users who will actually use the product.

#### 3. Focus on B2B
**Reasoning:** Career counselors, HR departments, and universities have budgets and procurement processes. One enterprise customer at $5K/year = 14 individual subscribers. Lower churn, higher LTV.

#### 4. Build API-First
**Reasoning:** Your APO calculation engine is unique IP. Selling API access to HR tech platforms (Workday, BambooHR, Greenhouse) could generate $10-50K MRR with minimal support overhead.
